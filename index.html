    How money really works

    Money is a human invention, and it has three big jobs. Tap each card to find out what it does.

      Where does money come from?

      Not a money tree, and mostly not a printing press either.

      1. 1

        Coins and notes

        The Royal Mint makes coins. The Bank of England makes banknotes. But cash is only a small slice of all the money in the UK.

      2. 2

        Banks create money by lending

        When a bank lends someone money for a house or a business, it types new money into their account. The Bank of England explains it this way too. Most money is created like this.

      3. 3

        Repaying makes it disappear

        When the loan is paid back, that money vanishes from the system. Money is constantly being created and destroyed.

      4. 4

        Someone keeps watch

        The Bank of England sets the main interest rate to stop prices rising too fast. Its target is for prices to rise about 2% a year.

      The Shrinking Basket

      Prices usually creep up every year. That's called inflation. It means money hidden under the bed slowly buys less. Move the slider and watch the basket empty.

              Golden rules of the Trading Post

              1. Both say yes. A trade only counts if both people agree, out loud, without pressure.
              2. Value it first. Before swapping, each person guesses the £ value of both sides. Check online or in a shop if you disagree.
              3. 24-hour cooling off. Either trader can undo a swap within a day. After that it's final. Real shops have similar rules.
              4. No trading shared things or things that belong to parents.
              5. Bigger trades need a contract. Write it down, both sign it, a parent witnesses it.

              Older learners: try inventing a family currency, like Somerset Shillings, and see what happens to prices when you print too many.

                UK rules for paid jobs

                Children can usually start part-time work at 14, or 13 in some council areas. Home-educated children of school age follow the same rules.

                • You may need a work permit from the council. Contact Somerset Council's education welfare service before starting any job.
                • No work in school hours, before 7am or after 7pm, or in factories and industrial sites.
                • Term time: up to 12 hours a week, including up to 2 hours on school days and Sundays. Saturdays: up to 5 hours at 14, 8 hours at 15–16.
                • School holidays: up to 25 hours a week at 14 and 35 hours at 15–16, with a 2-week break from work each year.
                • No National Minimum Wage until you are past school-leaving age. From 16, young-person rates apply.

                Source: GOV.UK, Restrictions on child employment. Rules and tax figures change, so always check GOV.UK.

                    Getting started

                      Safety rules

                      • An adult is present for any selling, door-to-door service or meeting customers.
                      • No online selling or payment apps without a parent-controlled account. Children never share bank or personal details.
                      • Real investing only through a Junior ISA or a parent-held account. Anything riskier uses a pretend portfolio.
                      • Talk about scams before any online mission, and use Agent Ava's checklist every time.
