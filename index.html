Money Quest Somerset

Learn maths with real money. Each week you learn one money idea, practise the sums behind it, then go out and use it in Somerset.

Your level

Choose your mentor

You can swap mentor any time. Your badges and passport stay with you.

How a week works

About 3 to 4 hours a week. Brothers and sisters share the same theme at their own level, so briefings, games and trips can be done together.

  1. MondayMission briefingYour mentor tells you the idea and a story.20 min
  2. TuesdayMaths workshopWorked examples and puzzles at your level.45 min
  3. WednesdayGame dayA board game, role-play shop or market simulation.40 min
  4. Thu to SatReal-world missionYour weekly homework, done out in the world.60–90 min
  5. SundayDebriefFill in your passport, get mentor feedback, check badges.20 min

    Somerset missions and trips

    One outing per quest, each with a job to do so the trip produces maths, not just a day out. Check opening times and market dates before you go.

    QuestWhereYour mission
    1. The Story of MoneyMuseum of Somerset, Taunton (home of the Frome Hoard of Roman coins)Estimate the hoard's value in today's money. Make a timeline of money from barter to apps.
    2. Smart SpendingA supermarket, then a farmers' market in Frome, Wells, Taunton or GlastonburyCompare unit prices. Ask a stallholder how they set their prices.
    3. Earning ItA Somerset farm shop, cheesemaker or cider farmFind out how a product gets from field to shelf. Work out costs, margins and seasonal income.
    3. Earning ItClarks Village and the shoe-making story in StreetLearn how one family business grew. Compare outlet prices with high-street prices.
    4. Growing ItYour bank or building society branch (book ahead)Ask about savings accounts for young people. Compare rates at home.
    5. Protecting ItYour local library or a Citizens Advice talkCollect leaflets on scams and consumer rights. Build a scam-spotting checklist.
    6. Giving and BuildingA village fete, Christmas fair or Frome Independent marketRun or help on a stall. Track takings, costs, profit and the share you give away.

    UK rules for paid jobs

    Children can usually start part-time work at 14, or 13 in some council areas. Home-educated children of school age follow the same rules.

    • You may need a work permit from the council. Contact Somerset Council's education welfare service before starting any job.
    • No work in school hours, before 7am or after 7pm, or in factories and industrial sites.
    • Term time: up to 12 hours a week, including up to 2 hours on school days and Sundays. Saturdays: up to 5 hours at 14, 8 hours at 15–16.
    • School holidays: up to 25 hours a week at 14 and 35 hours at 15–16, with a 2-week break from work each year.
    • No National Minimum Wage until you are past school-leaving age. From 16, young-person rates apply.

    Source: GOV.UK, Restrictions on child employment. Rules and tax figures change, so always check GOV.UK.

        For parents: Mission Control

        You voice the mentors, approve any mission with real money, and keep things safe. The children do the thinking.

        Getting started

          Safety rules

          • An adult is present for any selling, door-to-door service or meeting customers.
          • No online selling or payment apps without a parent-controlled account. Children never share bank or personal details.
          • Real investing only through a Junior ISA or a parent-held account. Anything riskier uses a pretend portfolio.
          • Talk about scams before any online mission, and use Agent Ava's checklist every time.
